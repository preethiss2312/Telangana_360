The Kakatiya dynasty ruled much of Telangana from Warangal, then called Orugallu, between the 12th and early 14th centuries. Famous rulers include Ganapati Deva, Rani Rudrama Devi and Prataparudra. The dynasty ended in the early 14th century when Delhi Sultanate forces conquered Warangal.

Ramappa Temple at Palampet in Mulugu district was built in 1213 by Recherla Rudra, a general of Ganapati Deva. It is famous for its carved stone sculptures and for its light bricks. UNESCO declared it a World Heritage Site in 2021.

The Thousand Pillar Temple at Hanamkonda was built by the Kakatiya king Rudra Deva in 1163. Warangal Fort, with its large stone gateways called kirti toranas, is another major Kakatiya monument.

The Qutb Shahi dynasty ruled from Golconda from 1518 to 1687. Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah founded the city of Hyderabad in 1591 and built the Charminar in the same year. Mecca Masjid was started in 1617 and completed under Aurangzeb in 1694. Golconda was known for its diamonds, and the Qutb Shahi tombs are near the fort.

Mughal emperor Aurangzeb conquered Golconda in 1687. After that, the Mughal governor Mir Qamar-ud-din Khan became the founder of the Asaf Jahi Nizam dynasty in 1724.

Bathukamma is the floral festival of Telangana. Women stack seasonal flowers in a tall cone, sing songs and immerse the Bathukamma in water during the nine days before Dussehra. Bonalu is a festival in honour of goddess Mahakali, celebrated in Hyderabad and Secunderabad in the month of Ashada.

The Sammakka Saralamma Jatara at Medaram in Mulugu district is held once in two years and is one of the largest tribal festivals in the world. It honours the tribal mother and daughter Sammakka and Saralamma, and millions of devotees attend.

Hyderabadi biryani is the best-known dish of Telangana. Hyderabadi haleem received a Geographical Indication tag in 2010. Traditional Telangana foods include sarva pindi, sakinalu, jonna rotte and pachi pulusu. Osmania biscuits, Irani chai and Qubani ka meetha are popular in Hyderabad.

Pochampally Ikat sarees from Bhoodan Pochampally, and Gadwal sarees, are famous handloom products of Telangana. Nirmal paintings and toys, and Cheriyal scroll paintings are well-known crafts. Perini Shivatandavam is a dance of the Kakatiya period that was revived in modern times.

Kaloji Narayana Rao, Dasarathi Krishnamacharyulu and C. Narayana Reddy are well-known Telugu writers from Telangana. Burrakatha and Oggu Katha are traditional folk storytelling forms of the region. Hyderabad's mixed Hindu and Muslim cultural life is often called the Ganga-Jamuni tehzeeb.
