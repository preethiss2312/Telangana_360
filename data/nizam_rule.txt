The Nizams of Hyderabad belonged to the Asaf Jahi dynasty. Mir Qamar-ud-din Khan, known as Asaf Jah I and Nizam-ul-Mulk, founded the dynasty in 1724 as Mughal power weakened in the Deccan. Seven Nizams ruled Hyderabad State from 1724 until 1948.

Hyderabad State was the largest princely state in British India. It included the Telugu-speaking Telangana region, the Marathi-speaking Marathwada region and some Kannada-speaking districts. Hyderabad city was its capital.

Mir Osman Ali Khan, Asaf Jah VII, was the last Nizam of Hyderabad. He ruled from 1911 to 1948. He appeared on the cover of TIME magazine in 1937 as one of the richest men in the world.

Under the last Nizam, Hyderabad State had its own currency, postal system, railways and army. Osmania University was founded in 1918, and Osmania General Hospital, the High Court building and Begumpet Airport were developed in his time.

After the great Musi river flood of 1908, the Nizams built the Osman Sagar and Himayat Sagar reservoirs to protect Hyderabad city from floods and to supply drinking water. Nizam Sagar on the Manjira river was built in the 1920s for irrigation.

Salar Jung I was the prime minister of Hyderabad State from 1853 to 1883. He reformed the revenue system, courts, police and schools. The Salar Jung Museum in Hyderabad holds the collection of the Salar Jung family.

Under Nizam rule, large parts of Telangana were controlled by jagirdars, deshmukhs and landlords who held huge estates. Peasants suffered under heavy taxes and vetti, which was forced unpaid labour. Urdu was the official language and administration language, while Telugu, Marathi and Kannada were neglected in schools and offices.

When India became independent in 1947, the Nizam wanted Hyderabad to remain independent. He signed a Standstill Agreement with India in November 1947. The Razakars, a paramilitary force led by Qasim Razvi, supported the Nizam and committed violence against villagers and opponents.

Operation Polo was the Indian Army's police action against Hyderabad State. It began on 13 September 1948 and lasted five days. The Hyderabad army surrendered on 17 September 1948 and Hyderabad State joined the Indian Union. Major General J. N. Chaudhuri led the Indian forces and became military governor. Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel was India's Home Minister at that time.

After 1948 the Nizam stayed on as Rajpramukh, the constitutional head of Hyderabad State, from 1950 to 1956. M. K. Vellodi was the first chief minister, and Burgula Ramakrishna Rao became the first elected chief minister in 1952. The day of 17 September is observed in Telangana every year, and different governments have given it different names.
