The Andhra Mahasabha was founded in 1930 at Jogipet to promote Telugu language, culture and education in Hyderabad State. Over time it became more political, and communist leaders gained influence in it during the 1940s.

Komaram Bheem was a Gond tribal leader of the Adilabad region. He fought the Nizam's officials and forest exploitation with the slogan Jal, Jangal, Zameen (water, forest, land). He was killed in 1940 at Jodeghat, and he remains a symbol of tribal resistance in Telangana.

The Telangana Armed Struggle of 1946 to 1951 was a peasant uprising against the Nizam's rule, jagirdars and landlords. It was led largely by communists and the Andhra Mahasabha, and it spread across Nalgonda, Warangal and Khammam districts. Villagers formed sanghams, took back land and resisted vetti, the system of forced labour. The communist party withdrew the struggle in October 1951.

Chakali Ailamma, also called Chityala Ailamma, was a washerwoman who stood up against a powerful landlord and became a symbol of courage in the Telangana armed struggle. Other leaders of the struggle include Ravi Narayana Reddy and Baddam Yella Reddy, and Devulapalli Venkateswara Rao and Puchalapalli Sundarayya also took part.

The Mulki rules protected government jobs in Hyderabad State for locals. In 1952 students in Warangal and Hyderabad protested against outsiders taking jobs, in the Non-Mulki Go Back agitation. Police firing during these protests caused deaths.

The States Reorganisation Commission headed by Fazl Ali looked at the demand for linguistic states in 1955. It suggested keeping Telangana as a separate state for some years, with a review after the 1961 elections. Instead Andhra Pradesh was formed on 1 November 1956 by merging Telangana with Andhra State. A Gentlemen's Agreement of 1956 promised safeguards to Telangana in jobs, education and revenue.

The 1969 Jai Telangana agitation began over violations of the safeguards. Students, especially from Osmania University, led protests, and many people died in police firing. The Telangana Praja Samithi, led by M. Chenna Reddy, won most Telangana seats in the 1971 Lok Sabha election. The Eight Point Plan of 1969 and the Six Point Formula of 1973 were attempts to settle the demand without creating a new state.

K. Chandrashekar Rao formed the Telangana Rashtra Samithi (TRS) on 27 April 2001 to demand a separate Telangana state. In November 2009 he began a fast, and on 9 December 2009 the central government announced the process for forming Telangana. After protests in Andhra and Rayalaseema regions the announcement was put on hold on 23 December 2009.

The Srikrishna Committee was set up in 2010 and gave its report in December 2010. In 2011 the Telangana Joint Action Committee, chaired by Professor M. Kodandaram, led the Million March and the Sakala Janula Samme, a general strike of many employees and workers.

On 30 July 2013 the Congress Working Committee supported a separate Telangana. The Andhra Pradesh Reorganisation Act was passed by Parliament in February 2014. Telangana was formed on 2 June 2014 as India's 29th state, with Hyderabad as the joint capital for up to ten years. K. Chandrashekar Rao became the first chief minister. The TRS was renamed Bharat Rashtra Samithi in 2022. In December 2023 the Congress party won the assembly election, and A. Revanth Reddy became chief minister.
